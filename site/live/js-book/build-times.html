Chapter 8

Build times

RC1's release builds were the project's slowest loop. The walker's release build grew with the language: 65 s when the controls first landed (84263a4b), 686 s after the language gaps (f0837ff4), and 1,052 s for js-run at roc-apps' commit tagged rc1-slow-release-build, for about 10,000 lines of Roc, against under a second on dev (the tag's message). roc-apps' commit 762c7d3a, on the tier's gate while it was roc-apps', gives the JavaScript tier's release build as 11 to 17 minutes and puts most of the time in RC1's SpecConstr pass.

The VM's release build was 670 s in its first round (29cb46ec), when it still imported the walker's module for the value rules. After the value rules moved into a module of their own and the VM dropped its import of the walker (d8d6b7fe), it was 84 s (15686d94, d7ce2f2c), while js-run still took 481 s. The drop came after the split, and the commits leave the cause unstated. Later figures are 110 s after the collector (f9188b2f) and 91 s in js/README.md's status line.

The interpreter works RC1's release pipeline harder than most Roc projects do. Jack Scully profiled the compiler on this code at roc-apps' 762c7d3a and opened three pull requests against RC1's SpecConstr pass: #13408 puts cheap checks ahead of its inlining queries, #13409 restructures the walk those queries run, and #13410 stops a recursive function from unrolling into its own body. On his machine, js-run's release build took 271 s before them; each alone brought it to 166 s, 63 s and 83 s, and #13410 also ran fib.js 12% and loops.js 14% faster (the pull requests' descriptions). He put the three together at about 12 times faster for this repository's code specifically (Zulip, #off topic > LLVM). They are expected to merge after Roc 0.10, so every build time above is RC1's own.
