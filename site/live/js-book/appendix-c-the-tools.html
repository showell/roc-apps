llvm-mca and the cost tier §

The races run on a shared two-core virtual machine, and their timings move with its load. x86/cost.shale measures what the bytes alone decide: it compiles each race, disassembles the executable with objdump, and hands llvm-mca the fast path of a range of bytecode instructions. Run twice, it prints the same tables byte for byte (x86/COST.md, "How to run it"). LLVM stays outside the compiler: llvm-mca reads only the disassembly of the bytes js-x86 writes (07e599b).

Its first table explained a result the timings left open. A branch of milestone 6's work cut the SAT race's watch loop, the loop of its search that visits each clause watching a literal just made false, from 348 instructions to 222 on its fast path, and the race ran at about the same speed. Over that fast path the stores grew from 42 to 63, 61 of them to the frame, and llvm-mca put both builds' block throughput at 63.0 cycles a pass: about half of the instructions cut were tag tests and their branches, cheap and off the loop's bound (x86/COST.md). The next branch took the loop's frame stores from 61 to 21, and its throughput to 31.8 cycles (044070b).

The counter of “What the compiler leaves to an optimizer” is the tier's other half, and it is js-x86's own code. With counted=1, x86/cost.shale also hands llvm-mca every block that ran, and each block's count times its throughput, summed, is the run's predicted cycles, a model for comparing two builds of one race (x86/COST.md, "The counter"). A counted executable is a separate file from the race's, and runs 3 to 10 times as long.
