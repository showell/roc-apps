Chapter 7

Shapes

A far tree in the ride is a trunk under eight triangles. So before we draw a tree, we draw its two shapes: a triangle and a rectangle.

In Safari Ride, a shape is a value that describes something to paint. The first four tags of the Shapes module's Shape type are the kinds of region the ride paints, each a region of the screen with a fill:

Each tag carries a record, and every record ends with a fill, the paint for the inside. The ride draws a great many triangles, plenty of four-cornered things, a few round lights, and outlines traced from emoji and mountains, so those are its four regions. More tags follow them in the type, for other jobs, such as a line of text.

A triangle §

Here's a triangle:

And here's the code that makes it:

The value is a Triangle tag with its record. The record names three corners, first, second and third, so every triangle has exactly three, by construction. A corner is a Point.Point, a record with an x and a y. Point.at makes one from two plain numbers, measured from the top left of the picture, with y growing downward. The picture is 640 pixels wide and 324 tall.

Point.at turns each of those numbers into a Pixels, an opaque type around an F64, like Distance. The ride works in metres on the road and in pixels on the screen, and two types keep the two apart, so each number says which one it is. Roc is just as happy with plain F64s, if you prefer that style. In a program that moves between the road and the screen all the time, the types earn their keep.

The fill is green. RgbColor.of makes a color from 0x2e8b57, a number in hex, as 0x says: the six digits a web page writes after its #, and Brush.flat makes a fill of that one color.

A shape only describes. So what paints the triangle? The platform does. The picture is a small Roc app of its own, and it hands the platform a list of two shapes: the background, a rectangle that fills the picture, and then the triangle. The platform paints the list from first to last, so the triangle lands on the background.

Here's the function:

|{ top_left, width, height, fill }| takes the record apart, naming each of its fields. The body works out where the right and bottom edges are, with plus, which adds two Pixels. Then it gives the quad its four corners, clockwise from the top left. Each corner is a record of an x and a y, each a Pixels, which is all a Point.Point is. Point.at builds the same record from two plain numbers. Each side's two corners share an x or a y, so the sides are always square to the screen. The same picture app, showing its rectangle, hands the platform the background and then this quad.

That's both of a far tree's shapes. Next, we put them together, with functions of our own.
