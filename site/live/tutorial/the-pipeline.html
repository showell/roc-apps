Chapter 10

The pipeline

Every frame, every thing beside the road takes the same trip to the screen. In the “A far tree” chapter, TreeShapes.tree_sprite takes a setting and gives back a tree's sprite. This chapter follows the trip around that function: where the setting comes from, and where the sprite goes. A tree, a cow, a radio tower, the cat, the bird and the truck each start from a place on the road and end as a rendering, a sprite with a depth, in the frame's one list. The Pipeline module holds the trip's types and the functions that sight and render, so we call the trip the pipeline.

Here's the trip. The sections below follow its boxes, from Geom.Placed down:

Each kind has a sighting module, such as TreeSighting. It gathers the placed things of its kind on the road ahead, and hands each one to a Pipeline function that sights it.

Placed §

The trip starts from where a thing stands on the road:

road_point is the thing's place on the road, a RoadPoint from the “Types for places” chapter, and lift says how far off the ground it stands. kind says what the thing is. Its type is the type variable kind, so a placed tree is a Geom.Placed(Conifer), and a placed cow is a Geom.Placed(Billboard). The next chapter looks inside Conifer.

Sighted §

The screen shows what the camera sees. So for each frame, the pipeline sights every placed thing in play, and works out where it stands from the camera:

The Spot is the thing's place seen from the camera, as the “Types for places” chapter showed. The kind rides along as it was. This spot is the one tree_sprite took from its setting in the “A far tree” chapter.

Refused §

Some placed things stay off the screen, so a sighting function's answer is a Try, Roc's type for an answer that can fail. A Try is one of two tags: Ok, holding the sighting, or Err, holding a tag that says why the thing is refused. Here's the first sighting function, whole:

Its answer is a Try(Pipeline.Sighted(kind), [BehindCamera]): a sighting, or the one refusal it can give. The body finds where the thing's foot lies from the rider, and the refusal comes from the ? after RiderView.rider_point_ahead(rider_point). That call gives back a Try of its own. When it's an Err(BehindCamera), the ? ends sighting_of right there with that Err. When it's an Ok, the ? unwraps it, and foot is the point ahead. The last line wraps the spot and the kind in an Ok.

Pipeline's other sighting functions start from sighting_of and add tests of their own, so between them they refuse a thing with one of three tags:

Refusal Why
BehindCamera It stands behind the camera, or too close in front of it to show.
BelowHorizon The ground curves away with distance, so far enough off, the horizon hides all of it.
TooSmall It would stand too small on the screen to be worth showing.

The trees, the billboard critters, the cat and the bird are sighted by Pipeline.scenery_sighting_of, which takes all three tests. A radio tower and the truck are kept however small they look. So a tower is sighted by Pipeline.sighting_above_horizon_of, which skips the TooSmall test, and the truck by Pipeline.sighting_of, which takes only the BehindCamera test.

Here are some things from step 1,529, the frame in the picture, and what their sightings decide. right and forward place each thing's foot from the camera, in metres, and a forward below zero is behind it. The road bends, so a tree on a segment far ahead can stand far to one side:

The cat, frozen in the road 0.38 23.63 kept

A small tree far off sinks below the horizon, while a taller one just past it still shows its top. Distance counts the whole way, sideways too: the hidden tree stands about 1,500 metres from the camera, and the truck about 500. Of the 214 trees on the segments in view, 136 are kept, 30 are behind the camera, and 48 are below the horizon. Each sighting module keeps its sightings with keep_oks, a List function, which keeps the Ok values of a list of Trys and drops the Errs. The truck's module hands its one Try to Scene, which renders the truck only for an Ok.

Rendered §

Each sighting that's kept is rendered by Pipeline.rendering_of, which the “Asking a kind” chapter explains. It gives back a Rendered:

A Rendered holds a depth and a sprite, and those two alone. The :: makes it opaque, as it does Distance. The sprite is what a kind's scenery_sprite gives back from a setting, and the depth is how far ahead of the camera the thing stands. The kind has done its job by now, and it's gone: a tree's rendering and a cow's have the same type.

In a body, as here, Rendered.({ depth, sprite }) builds a Rendered by wrapping the record. Among a function's arguments, as Billboard.(billboard) was, the same form takes one apart.

One list §

Every rendering has that one type, so a frame gathers them all in one list:

A Scene holds a list of renderings for each sort of thing, and join makes one list of them all. The guard rail along the road makes its own Rendereds, as a later chapter shows, and scene.rails joins them to the list. Rendered.depth_sorted_renderings sorts the list from far to near, and the platform paints it in that order, so a near thing covers a far one.

So every thing beside the road makes the same trip: placed on the road, sighted by the camera, rendered as a sprite with a depth, and sorted into the frame's one list. Its kind rides through the first two types and is gone by the third. That leaves two questions, and the next two chapters answer them: what is a kind, and how does rendering_of, written once, get a sprite out of every kind?
