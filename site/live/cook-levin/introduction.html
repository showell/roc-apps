A riff on Cook-Levin Chapters

Chapter 1

Introduction

Steve Howell devised a virtual machine with a single two-bit register, AX, and four instructions: nada, zero, decr and mod2. Every program is exactly six of those instructions. A run starts with AX holding the input, a number from 0 to 3, and ends in one of three ways:

  • zero accepts the input, the moment it finds AX at 00;
  • decr rejects it, the moment it finds AX at 00 (otherwise it takes 1 from AX);
  • the run rejects implicitly when it gets past the sixth instruction with neither of them having seen 00.

nada leaves AX as it is, and mod2 takes 2 from AX when it is 2 or 3. So each program sorts the four inputs into accepted and rejected, and the accepted ones are the language the program decides. The machine is small enough that every program, every input and every step can be listed, 4,096 programs in all, so every claim about it can be checked by running all of them.

Steve wrote the first version by hand in Python, in virtual-machine: the machine (virtual_machine.py), a general polynomial library (poly.py) and a stepper that writes one step of the machine as polynomials (stepper.py). In the cook-levin-riff repository, the code was written by AI coding agents (Claude), with human supervision, as its README.md says in its second line.

What the book covers §

“The machine” sets out the machine, and “Languages and machines” gives the computer science behind the word "language", back to Turing. “Programs as languages” is the search over all 4,096 programs, and “Nibble, the puzzle page” turns its 16 languages into puzzles. “Arithmetization” turns one step into polynomials. “Why composing blew up” and “Six steps composed” take the step through a whole program, and “The polynomials, in full” prints every one of those polynomials whole, the Python's among them. “Cook-Levin as SAT” turns the same machine into a formula for a SAT solver. “The oracles” lines up every implementation that computes the answers. “The Roc port” reads the mathematics in Roc. “What is Shale?” describes the scripting language every script here is written in, and “The VM in Shale” reads the machine and the composition in it.

Why the name fits §

The Cook-Levin theorem says that satisfiability of Boolean formulas is NP-complete: any problem whose answers a machine can check in polynomial time reduces to it. The proof unrolls the checking machine, step by step, into one formula. This repository does that unrolling on a toy instrument. It makes no claim about NP, and it reduces one machine's questions, never an arbitrary verifier's. It also goes one step past the formula, into arithmetic: the whole program becomes four polynomials with integer coefficients. "A riff" is the honest word for that.

Where the numbers come from §
