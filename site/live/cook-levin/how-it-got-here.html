Chapter 2

How it got here

This chapter is the short history, as two git logs record it.

The Python, 2023 §

The virtual-machine log opens on 2023-03-11 with f01c1c3 and runs through 158 commits to 2023-04-08, all under Steve's address. The stepper arrived on its first day (ba9388a), the polynomial library from 2023-03-12 (6bb7429), and the step polynomials on 2023-03-14 (a4e4765), the same day as MAX_STEPS of 6. The last 2023 commits, on 2023-04-08, begin with "Eliminate most poly-related stuff" (72bf4c4).

The composition that stalled, 2026 §

Five commits marked "wip" followed on 2026-01-28. On 2026-04-16 a measuring harness, compose.py, was added with Claude as co-author (1a5f6fa). Its message says that step 1 composed in about 0.9 s and that step 2 ran past 30 s in Python, and that those numbers motivated a port to Go. The same afternoon compose.py was removed, "porting six-step composition to Go instead" (a2406b5). The next day an essay Claude had written about the project was parked as DEEP_READ.md (94c3d31); it lists the six-step composition as the step the project stops before.

The Go port lives in a repository of its own, outside this record. By Steve's account it drowned in big integers; that account is unchecked here.

The finish, 2026-11-25 §

The cook-levin-riff log covers one day. The C port of the machine and the search came first (8051ce2), then the general polynomial (ccde4f8) and the stepper (5b14d74). The Boolean ring came at 15:59 (0c233e3), and all six steps composed, proved on all 2^14 points, at 16:23 (1146d43). The Nibble page (b1fe11f), the Roc port (6206236), the SAT encoding (8de51b7), rocsat as an oracle (f977bcc) and the JavaScript port (e996749, c251895) followed. The first commit is stamped 15:32 and the last 19:21.
