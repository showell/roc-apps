Chapter 41

How it got here

Every frame. Tools and history, safe to skip; it stands alone. We tell how Steve built the toy in four days of March 2023, how far his polynomials got, and the afternoon in 2026 when all six steps finally composed.

This book takes its ideas in the order that explains them best, which is seldom the order they came in. This chapter gives the order they came in, as two git logs record it.

Four days in March 2023 §

The virtual-machine log records the first four days:

  1. 2023-03-11, 01:49: the machine, run_progam, and the search find_solutions, in the first commit (f01c1c3).
  2. 13:02 the same day: the analytical solution (d6bb78f), as “Programs as formal languages” tells.
  3. 15:47 the same day: the stepper (ba9388a), with its NOT, AND and OR already arithmetic on integers (NOT x was (x − 1)·(x − 1) then), and the assertion that the stepper accepts exactly the formal language's inputs.
  4. 2023-03-12: mod2 and pass, today's nada (cad8e18), then the polynomial library from 14:35 (6bb7429).
  5. 2023-03-14: the step polynomials at 12:22 (a4e4765), and at 12:32 programs of six lines, with the assertion that every formal language has a program (8646cbe).

From the machine Steve went straight to polynomials, writing its logic as arithmetic on integers so that a whole run could become polynomials. The plain Boolean view of the toy, its formal languages and its step written with ordinary logic gates, sits between the two, and the order passed over it; “The Boolean view” sets it out.

A successful monstrosity, 2023 §

The log runs through 158 commits to 2023-04-08, all under Steve's address. The polynomial stunt got far. Steve's Python built one step of the machine as four polynomials in the general ring, where x·x is x², and ran programs through them: test_with_stepper evaluates them once a line, six times a run, and the search checks each formal language's example program that way. The polynomials worked, at 786 terms for hb' and 2,979 for lb'. Composing them, substituting each step into the next so that the whole program is one polynomial, stayed out of reach. The last 2023 commits begin with "Eliminate most poly-related stuff" (72bf4c4).

The composition that stalled, 2026 §

Five commits marked "wip" followed on 2026-01-28. On 2026-04-16 a measuring harness, compose.py, was added with Claude as co-author (1a5f6fa). Its message says that step 1 composed in about 0.9 s and that step 2 ran past 30 s in Python, and that those numbers motivated a port to Go. The same afternoon compose.py was removed, "porting six-step composition to Go instead" (a2406b5). The next day an essay Claude had written about the project was parked as DEEP_READ.md (94c3d31); it lists the six-step composition as the step the project stops before.

The Go port lives in a repository of its own, outside this record. By Steve's account it drowned in big integers; that account is unchecked here.

The finish, 2026-11-25 §

The cook-levin-riff log opens on 2026-11-25. The Boolean ring was reached for to make the calculation tractable:

2026-11-25 what landed commit
15:32 the C port of the machine and the search 8051ce2
then the general polynomial ccde4f8
then the stepper 5b14d74
15:59 the Boolean ring, where x² = x 0c233e3
16:23 all six steps composed, proved on all 2^14 points 1146d43
later that day the Nibble page b1fe11f
later that day the Roc port 6206236
later that day the SAT encoding 8de51b7
later that day rocsat as an oracle f977bcc

Fifty-one minutes after the C port's first commit, the composition Steve had stopped short of in 2023 was done.
