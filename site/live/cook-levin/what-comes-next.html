Chapter 43

What comes next

Every frame. Tools and history; it stands alone. We list the questions still open, say how to start on your own, and name three ways to lighten the solver's work.

A few questions stand open:

  • A conjecture about half. Over {zero, decr, mod2} and over {zero, decr}, half seems to help the same 38,911 formal languages; the census checks it on every one, and it awaits a proof (“Shortest programs with mod2 and half”).
  • SOLVABLE as the machine grows. For each fixed width it is in P; whether it stays in P as the width grows is open (“Nibble, sized”).
  • Nondeterminism. The machine is deterministic. The SAT encoding already treats the program as the unknown, which is the theorem's "a certificate exists"; a machine with choice instructions would make the riff closer to the theorem.

You can use any mainstream programming language to study the toy programming languages. js/cook_levin.js is the machine, the search and the six-step composition in plain JavaScript, and it prints the same report and composition byte for byte under node, and also as an x86-64 program compiled by js-x86, a compiler we wrote ourselves, in Roc, in roc-js-interpreter.

Ways we could make the solver's work lighter §

Each idea here holds for every puzzle alike, so the solver still finds each puzzle's answer itself, and each would help any solver of rocsat's kind.

Symmetry breaking. Many programs are twins: nada leaves the machine as it was, so moving a program's nada lines to its end keeps what it decides. Clauses that say "a nada line is followed by nada", two for each pair of neighbouring lines, keep one twin of each family, and the solver searches fewer programs. sat/walkthrough.shale counts them over all 4,096:

line 1 nada gives line 2 nada: (x1 OR x2 OR NOT x3), (x1 OR x2 OR NOT x4)
the 10 clauses for lines 1 to 6 keep 1093 of the 4096 programs
formal languages decided: 16 by all 4096, 16 by the 1093 kept
{0, 2, 3} keeps its one program, 1689

Each SATISFIABLE answer stays SATISFIABLE, and each UNSATISFIABLE one stays UNSATISFIABLE. The count of solutions changes, so the oracle's count of programs would keep the formula as it is.

Better encodings. A smaller step shrinks all 24 of its copies at once. The encoder already folds constants and shares gates, so a puzzle's formula has 404 gates where 24 steppers of 34 would have 816 (“The Cook-Levin machine”), and sat_step's 34 AND and OR gates are fewer than the mux circuit's 43 (“Appendix: the step's encodings”). Each AND or OR gate saved is three clauses fewer in every copy of the step that builds it.

Fewer inputs at a time. “Guided search” asks about a few inputs at once and adds an input each round the found program gets it wrong, so each round asks the solver to satisfy a few of the 16 runs.
