A riff on Cook-Levin Chapters

Chapter 29

The program left unknown

Now we make the move of “The Cook-Levin machine” again, on the polynomial road: leave the program's bits unknown, and the machine becomes a formula whose true points are exactly the programs that solve a puzzle. Then we compare the two roads, naming in Part III and expanding in this part.

“The Boolean view” ran the machine as four Boolean functions of six bits, composed over six steps, with the input and the program fixed. Cook and Levin's move is to stop fixing the program. Keep the same four functions and the same six steps, give the program's twelve bits names in place of values, v1 to v12, and what comes out of the sixth step is a formula where it was a bit. “The Cook-Levin machine” made this move inside a circuit, for a solver; here the step functions take the unknowns themselves, which lets Part IV expand them into polynomials. This chapter leaves the program unknown and turns the formula into the question a puzzle asks.

Each input fixed §

Line k's opcode bits are v(2k − 1) and v(2k), so instruction k is 2·v(2k − 1) + v(2k), numbered as in “Cook-Levin as SAT”. The input stays fixed, so hb and lb start as constants and both flags as 0. python/steps.py composes the six steps with the same Python functions, now given formulas: a constant folds away, and an equal subformula is built once. The final accepted, for each input, has this many gates:

For input 0 it is small enough to read:

   (NOT v1 AND v2)
OR (NOT (v1 AND NOT v2) AND NOT v3 AND v4)
OR (NOT ((v1 AND NOT v2) OR (v3 AND NOT v4)) AND NOT v5 AND v6)
OR (NOT ((v1 AND NOT v2) OR (v3 AND NOT v4) OR (v5 AND NOT v6)) AND NOT v7 AND v8)
OR (NOT ((v1 AND NOT v2) OR (v3 AND NOT v4) OR (v5 AND NOT v6) OR (v7 AND NOT v8)) AND NOT v9 AND v10)
OR (NOT ((v1 AND NOT v2) OR (v3 AND NOT v4) OR (v5 AND NOT v6) OR (v7 AND NOT v8) OR (v9 AND NOT v10)) AND NOT v11 AND v12)

Line k's bits 0 1 are zero, and 1 0 are decr. So the formula says: some line is zero, and every line before it is nada, zero or mod2. That is input 0's whole story, since AX starts at 0: zero accepts it, decr rejects it, and nada and mod2 leave it as it is. python/steps.py holds all four formulas to the interpreter on all 4,096 programs.

Composition §

Free the input's two bits as well. The first step reads them as hb and lb, both flags at 0, and line 1's two bits. Each later step reads the four bits the one before it wrote, and its own line's two bits. So the only bits that enter from outside the chain are the input's two and each line's two, six lines of them:

bits
the input, hb and lb at the start 2
the flags at the start 0 (both constant 0)
the program, two a line for six lines 12
the composed expression's variables 14

Everything else is internal: each step's gates, and of the 24 state bits the steps write, the 20 that a next step reads. After the sixth step, accepted is an expression accepted(n, v) of those 14 bits.

The question as one expression §

A puzzle asks whether a program decides L. That is one more expression, of the twelve program bits alone:

decides_L(v1, ..., v12) = AND over n in 0..3 of (accepted(n, v) == L(n))

L(n) is the constant 1 or 0, so each of the four comparisons is accepted(n, v) or its complement. The true points of decides_L are exactly the programs that decide L.

The payoff §

Expanded and simplified, decides_L is a sum of products over v1 to v12. src/boolean_main.c computes one for every formal language: it finds L's programs by running all 4,096, takes the prime implicants of that set, and covers it greedily, the product holding the most programs left taken first. Each product is written as its twelve bits, two a line of the program: 1 for v, 0 for its complement, and - for a bit the product leaves free. Two ways to compose §

The composing above ran all 4,096 programs, which a toy allows. For a real checker there are two ways to compose the step's expressions.

  • Expand it. Substitute each step's expressions into the next and multiply them out, into one big expression of the fourteen bits. “Arithmetization” does this with the logic written as arithmetic on integers, AND as a product and the complement of x as 1 − x, which makes each expression a polynomial; that move is called arithmetization. “Six steps composed” expands all six, to 10,324 terms for accepted. The size grows with every step substituted.
  • Name it. Give each intermediate wire a fresh variable, and state only how it relates to its inputs. That is Tseitin's encoding and SAT, the road “Cook-Levin as SAT” took: 416 variables and 1,216 clauses for each puzzle, a count that grows linearly with the steps, one stepper's gates per step.

Naming is why Cook-Levin's formula stays polynomial in size: each step adds the same few clauses, whatever came before it. Expanding is the riff's own road, and the rest of this part follows it to see how far it goes.
