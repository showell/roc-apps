Chapter 14

Cook-Levin as SAT

src/sat.c writes each puzzle as a Boolean formula in DIMACS CNF, the input format of SAT solvers (8de51b7). This is the Cook-Levin construction itself: the question "which six-instruction programs accept exactly this language?" becomes one formula whose satisfying assignments are exactly those programs.

The unrolling §

The formula's first 12 variables are the program's bits, two an instruction, so a solver's model reads straight off as a program. The machine is then unrolled: each of the six steps, on each of the four inputs, is a copy of the step's logic, wired to the state the step before left. The logic is the stepper's, gate for gate, NOT, AND and OR as in “Arithmetization”.

Each AND and OR becomes a Tseitin variable, tied to its inputs by three clauses, and a NOT is a literal's sign:

A gate on a constant is folded, and a gate already built from the same inputs is reused, so every puzzle's formula has 416 variables and 1,216 clauses. out/tcc/sat facts, run for this book, prints those two numbers for all 16. The last four clauses ask that each input's final accepted be what the language wants. len=N forces the lines past N to nada, and fix=K:OP fixes line K, as the puzzle page does.

The proof by propagation §

tests/test_sat.c proves the encoding by itself. For every puzzle it sets the program bits to each of the 4,096 programs and unit-propagates them through the formula. Each gate's inputs come before it, so propagation alone decides every Tseitin variable. The assignments that survive are exactly the programs the machine says solve the puzzle, 4,096 over the 16 languages. With lines past N forced to nada, the formula is satisfiable exactly when N is at least the puzzle's shortest length.

rocsat as an oracle §

sat/oracle.shale hands the formulas to rocsat, Matt Pizenberg's CDCL SAT solver, written in Roc (f977bcc). For each puzzle it solves the formula and runs the model's program on the machine. It counts the solutions by adding a clause that blocks each model and solving again until rocsat says UNSAT, and holds that count to the machine's. Then it proves the shortest length: one line shorter is UNSAT, and the shortest length itself is SAT.

An UNSAT answer claims that the formula is unsatisfiable, and rocsat backs each one with a DRAT proof, a list of clause additions and deletions that a separate checker can replay. Here an UNSAT at length N - 1 says that every program of N - 1 lines fails the puzzle, and the oracle checks its proof with the repository's own checker, src/drat.c, as “The solver's work, in full” shows. The oracle starts rocsat 4,143 times, so it takes about a minute and runs as its own script, sat/oracle.shale, beside build.shale's quicker tiers. Its last run for this book, on 2026-11-25, spent 62.6 s in its programs and ended "rocsat agrees with the machine on all 16 puzzles": 4,096 models in all, and every shortest length confirmed by an UNSAT one line shorter, with all 15 proofs verified.
