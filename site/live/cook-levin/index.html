A riff on Cook-Levin

A small book about cook-levin-riff: a toy machine whose every program is a polynomial and a Boolean formula, the six-step composition that took three years to arrive, and five independent ways of computing the same answers that all agree.

The book is meant to be skimmed. Each chapter opens with a guidepost, in bold. It starts with the chapter's frame, the way it looks at the machine: ordinary computation, circuit design, mathematics, complexity theory or SAT solving. Where a chapter changes frame partway through, a line in italics says so. Then comes the chapter's kind, one of six, and the earlier chapter it needs, so you can jump in anywhere:

  • Start here: the pitch and the machine, the two chapters to read first.
  • A review: well-known ideas, retold beside the toy; skip one you know.
  • New results: theorems, counts and answers worked out for this book, each checked by a run.
  • The machine shown another way: the same machine as a puzzle, as gates, a circuit, a formula, clauses or polynomials, with program 1689 shown in each.
  • Big output to skim: a monstrosity, printed whole because its size is the point.
  • Tools and history: the languages, the solver, the cross-checks, the story, and what is still open.

Part IV, the polynomials:

  • “The program left unknown”: the machine as a formula of its program's bits, and two roads forward. The machine shown another way.
  • “Arithmetization”: logic turned into arithmetic, kept small by one identity, x² = x. The machine shown another way.
  • “Six steps composed”: four polynomials that are the machine. New results.
  • “The delightful monstrosity”: the machine as one polynomial, in full view, then evaluated by hand. Big output to skim.

Part V, Nibble++, a bigger machine:

  • “Nibble++”: a bigger machine, and why we built it. New results.
  • “Two theorems about Nibble++”: Fibonacci numbers, off by one where the inputs end. New results.
  • “Shortest programs with mod2 and half”: every shortest length, explained by one recursion. New results.
  • “Nibble++ meets rocsat”: a 17 MB proof, replayed. New results.
  • “is_not_14 needs 28 lines”: a public question, answered twice. New results.
  • “Guided search”: a search that asks about a few inputs at a time. New results.

Part VI, the cross-checks, and the history:

  • “The oracles”: five ways to the same answers. Tools and history.
  • “The Roc port”: the mathematics as Roc writes it. Tools and history.
  • “How it got here”: the toy in four days of 2023, and its polynomials composed in 2026. Tools and history.
  • “What the toy taught us”: Cook-Levin as it looks from inside the toy, in one page. A review.
  • “What comes next”: the steps still open. Tools and history.
  • “Appendix: two DRAT proofs”: two of those proofs, printed lemma by lemma. Big output to skim.
