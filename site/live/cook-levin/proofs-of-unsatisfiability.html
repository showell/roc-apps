Chapter 26

Proofs of unsatisfiability

SAT solving. A review; it needs “Cook-Levin as SAT”. We show what a DRAT proof is on a formula of four clauses you can check with a pencil, and why an UNSATISFIABLE answer needs a proof when a SATISFIABLE one needs only an assignment.

SATISFIABLE is checked, UNSATISFIABLE is proved §

A SATISFIABLE answer carries its own evidence, the assignment, and rocsat check tests it clause by clause, in time that grows in step with the formula's length. That is NP's verifier (“P and NP”), and it runs in polynomial time. Finding the assignment is the hard part. The plain way tries all 2^n assignments of n variables: 4,096 for a puzzle's 12 program bits, since propagation settles its other 404 variables, and 2^56 for the 56 program bits of is_not_14 at 28 lines. rocsat prunes most of each search away, yet every method known takes exponential time on some formulas; whether some method could always take polynomial time is the P versus NP question. An UNSATISFIABLE answer needs evidence of another kind, a proof that every assignment breaks a clause, and DRAT is the format rocsat writes it in.

Lemmas, by RUP §

A DRAT proof is a list of clauses, its lemmas, each a consequence of the formula and the lemmas before it, ending at the empty clause (), which is false under every assignment. A checker tests each lemma by reverse unit propagation, RUP: suppose every literal of the lemma false, propagate, and look for a conflict. A conflict shows that the lemma holds wherever the formula and the lemmas before it hold.

Here is a formula small enough to check by hand, sat/walkthrough/no_opcode.cnf: line 1's two bits, with each of the four opcodes ruled out by one clause.

clause rules out its bits
(hb OR lb) nada 00
(hb OR NOT lb) zero 01
(NOT hb OR lb) decr 10
(NOT hb OR NOT lb) mod2 11 
-- line 1 of a program, with each of its four opcodes ruled out
hb = x1
lb = x2
2 variables, 4 clauses
(hb OR lb)
(hb OR NOT lb)
(NOT hb OR lb)
(NOT hb OR NOT lb)

Line 1 has to be some opcode, so the formula is unsatisfiable. Its proof, no_opcode.drat, is four lines, and sat/walkthrough.shale replays each by propagation of its own:

line 1, `1 0`: the lemma (hb)
  suppose hb = false
  lb = true, from (hb OR lb)
  conflict: every literal of (hb OR NOT lb) is false
  so (hb) follows by RUP
line 2, `d 1 2 0`: delete (hb OR lb)
line 3, `d 1 -2 0`: delete (hb OR NOT lb)
line 4, `0`: the lemma ()
  hb = true, from (hb)
  lb = true, from (NOT hb OR lb)
  conflict: every literal of (NOT hb OR NOT lb) is false
  so () follows by RUP

The lemma (hb) says line 1 is decr or mod2, since the first two clauses ruled out nada and zero. With hb known, the last two clauses clash, and the empty clause follows. The repository's checker, src/drat.c, agrees: verified: 2 lemmas (0 by RAT), 2 deletions, over 2 variables and 4 clauses; the empty clause at line 4.

The shortest proof is the empty clause alone, a file of one line, 0. It suffices when propagation from the formula's own clauses reaches a conflict, as in the false case of “Naming the gates”: sat/walkthrough/circuit_false.drat is that line, beside the circuit's CNF with its three inputs fixed, and the checker accepts it.

Deletions §

A line that starts with d deletes a clause from the checker's database. Above, once (hb) is known, it satisfies (hb OR lb) and (hb OR NOT lb) for good, and deleting them saves propagation the work of visiting them. Deleting only weakens the database, so each later lemma still follows from the formula. Deletions are a solver's housekeeping, and they keep a long proof fast to check: the proof that five lines are too few for {0, 2, 3} has 286 lemmas and 387 deletions (“The solver's work, in full”).

RAT, briefly §

RAT stands for resolution asymmetric tautology. An asymmetric tautology is a clause RUP accepts. Resolution joins two clauses that clash on one variable, (l OR A) and (NOT l OR B), into (A OR B). A lemma is RAT on its first literal l when every clause holding the complement of l, resolved with the lemma, gives a clause RUP accepts. That lets a proof name a fresh variable m: (m OR NOT hb OR NOT lb) is RAT on m, since the database holds NOT m in zero clauses, and (NOT m OR hb) and (NOT m OR lb) follow, Tseitin's three clauses for m = hb AND lb, made inside a proof. src/drat.c tries RAT on a lemma's first literal when RUP fails. All 15 of rocsat's puzzle proofs pass by RUP alone, so RAT appears in this book in this paragraph.

A SATISFIABLE answer is an assignment you check, and an UNSATISFIABLE one is a proof you replay; here both fit on a page.
