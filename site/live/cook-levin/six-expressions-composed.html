A riff on Cook-Levin Chapters

Chapter 12

Six expressions, composed

“Some history of the approach” showed each language as a Boolean function of two bits. The machine's step is one too, of six bits, and six steps composed are one of fourteen. This chapter writes the step as plain Boolean expressions, composes them into the question a puzzle asks, and then names the two ways of composing that the rest of this part follows.

The step as expressions §

A step reads hb, lb, halted, accepted, op_hb and op_lb, as in “Arithmetization”, and writes the next four. Eight small facts about the machine come first, then the four next-state bits, each read with the facts above it (src/decides.c):

ax_is_0 = NOT hb AND NOT lb
ax_is_2 = hb AND NOT lb
ax_is_3 = hb AND lb
is_zero = NOT op_hb AND op_lb
is_decr = op_hb AND NOT op_lb
is_mod2 = op_hb AND op_lb
runs = NOT halted
keeps = halted OR NOT op_hb
hb' = (hb AND keeps) OR (ax_is_3 AND is_decr AND runs)
lb' = (lb AND (keeps OR (is_mod2 AND runs))) OR (ax_is_2 AND is_decr AND runs)
halted' = halted OR (ax_is_0 AND is_decr)
accepted' = accepted OR (ax_is_0 AND is_zero AND runs)

keeps says that AX stays as it is: the machine has halted, or the line is nada or zero. So hb' is the old hb kept, or a decr from 3 to 2. lb' is the old lb kept, or kept by mod2, which keeps lb and clears hb, or a decr from 2 to 1. halted' adds a decr at 0, and accepted' a zero at 0 on a running machine. tests/test_boolean.c holds the four to src/stepper.c's step on all 64 settings of the six bits, and src/boolean_main.c checks them again before it prints them for this book. They are the gates of sat_step in “The Cook-Levin machine”, merged by hand.

Composition §

Chain six copies. The first reads the input's two bits, both flags at 0 and line 1's two bits; each later copy reads the state the one before it wrote, and its own line's bits. After the sixth, accepted is an expression accepted(n, v) of 2 + 12 bits: the input's two and the program's twelve, v1 to v12, numbered as in “Cook-Levin as SAT”, where instruction i is 2·v(2i − 1) + v(2i).

The question as one expression §

A puzzle asks whether a program decides L. That is one more expression, of the twelve program bits alone:

decides_L(v1, ..., v12) = AND over n in 0..3 of (accepted(n, v) == L(n))

L(n) is the constant 1 or 0, so each of the four comparisons is accepted(n, v) or its complement. The true points of decides_L are exactly the programs that decide L.

The payoff §

Expanded and simplified, decides_L is a sum of products over v1 to v12. src/boolean_main.c computes one for every language: it finds the programs that decide L by running all 4,096, takes the prime implicants of that set, and covers it greedily, the product holding the most programs left taken first. Each product is written as its twelve bits, two a line of the program: 1 for v, 0 for its complement, and - for a bit the product leaves free. For {0, 2, 3} the expression is one product, the conjunction of 12 literals that spells its one program, zero, decr, decr, zero, decr, zero:

01 10 10 01 10 01

For {0, 3}, 12 programs take 8 products. The first is zero, decr, decr, decr, zero with line 6 free:

01 10 10 10 01 --
0- 01 10 10 10 01
01 10 10 00 10 01
01 10 00 10 10 01
01 0- 10 10 10 01
01 10 10 11 10 01
01 10 10 10 -1 01
01 10 10 10 0- 01

Two ways to compose §

The composing above ran all 4,096 programs, which a toy allows. For a real checker there are two ways to compose the step's expressions, and the rest of this part takes each in turn.

  • Expand it. Substitute each step's expressions into the next and multiply out. That is arithmetization: “Arithmetization” writes the step as polynomials over the integers, and “Six steps composed” expands all six, to 10,324 terms for accepted. The size grows with every step substituted.
  • Name it. Give each intermediate wire a fresh variable, and state only how it relates to its inputs. That is Tseitin's encoding and SAT: “Cook-Levin as SAT” has 416 variables and 1,216 clauses for each puzzle, and the count grows linearly with the steps, one step unit's gates per step.

Naming is why Cook-Levin's formula stays polynomial in size: each step adds the same few clauses, whatever came before it.
