Chapter 13

Six expressions, composed

“The Boolean view” wrote one step of the machine as twelve Boolean statements of six bits. Six steps composed are one function of fourteen bits: the input's two and the program's twelve. This chapter composes them into the question a puzzle asks, and then names the two ways of composing that the rest of this part follows.

Composition §

Chain six copies. The first reads the input's two bits as hb and lb, both flags at 0, and line 1's two bits. Each later copy reads the four primed bits the one before it wrote, and its own line's two bits. So the only bits that enter from outside the chain are the input's two and each line's two, six lines of them:

bits
the input, hb and lb at the start 2
the flags at the start 0 (both constant 0)
the program, two a line for six lines 12
the composed expression's variables 14

Everything else is internal: six steps of eight facts each, and of the 24 primed bits, the 20 that the next step reads. After the sixth step, accepted' is an expression accepted(n, v) of those 14 bits. The program's bits are v1 to v12, numbered as in “Cook-Levin as SAT”, where instruction i is 2·v(2i − 1) + v(2i).

The question as one expression §

A puzzle asks whether a program decides L. That is one more expression, of the twelve program bits alone:

decides_L(v1, ..., v12) = AND over n in 0..3 of (accepted(n, v) == L(n))

L(n) is the constant 1 or 0, so each of the four comparisons is accepted(n, v) or its complement. The true points of decides_L are exactly the programs that decide L.

The payoff §

Expanded and simplified, decides_L is a sum of products over v1 to v12. src/boolean_main.c computes one for every problem: it finds the programs that decide L by running all 4,096, takes the prime implicants of that set, and covers it greedily, the product holding the most programs left taken first. Each product is written as its twelve bits, two a line of the program: 1 for v, 0 for its complement, and - for a bit the product leaves free. For {0, 2, 3} the expression is one product, the conjunction of 12 literals that spells its one program, zero, decr, decr, zero, decr, zero:

01 10 10 01 10 01

For {0, 3}, 12 programs take 8 products. The first is zero, decr, decr, decr, zero with line 6 free:

01 10 10 10 01 --
0- 01 10 10 10 01
01 10 10 00 10 01
01 10 00 10 10 01
01 0- 10 10 10 01
01 10 10 11 10 01
01 10 10 10 -1 01
01 10 10 10 0- 01

Two ways to compose §

The composing above ran all 4,096 programs, which a toy allows. For a real checker there are two ways to compose the step's expressions, and the rest of this part takes each in turn.

  • Expand it. Substitute each step's expressions into the next and multiply them out, into one big expression of the fourteen bits. “Arithmetization” does this with the logic written as arithmetic on integers, AND as a product and the complement of x as 1 − x, which makes each expression a polynomial; that move is called arithmetization. “Six steps composed” expands all six, to 10,324 terms for accepted. The size grows with every step substituted.
  • Name it. Give each intermediate wire a fresh variable, and state only how it relates to its inputs. That is Tseitin's encoding and SAT: “Cook-Levin as SAT” has 416 variables and 1,216 clauses for each problem, and the count grows linearly with the steps, one step unit's gates per step.

Naming is why Cook-Levin's formula stays polynomial in size: each step adds the same few clauses, whatever came before it.
