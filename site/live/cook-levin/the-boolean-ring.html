Chapter 31

The Boolean ring

Mathematics. Cool math; it needs “Arithmetization”. We show you the one identity, x² = x, that keeps every polynomial here multilinear, so that composing six steps takes a fraction of a second.

A whole program is six steps. Composing them means substituting: step 1's four polynomials go in for the state variables of step 2, and so on, until the final state is four polynomials in the 14 bits of “Six expressions, composed”. One polynomial would then hold the whole machine.

x² = x §

Every variable here is only ever 0 or 1, and on those values x² = x. A ring where every x² is x is a Boolean ring: every polynomial in it is multilinear, each variable to the first power at most. A monomial is then a set of variables, kept as a bit mask, and the product of two monomials is the union of their sets, the OR of their masks.

Why it is exact §

Two polynomials that agree on every 0/1 point reduce to the same multilinear polynomial, and a multilinear polynomial is fixed by its values on {0, 1}^n. So reducing by x² = x keeps every value the machine can see, and the canonical form makes == equality of functions (roc/BoolPoly.roc's header).

Each substitution keeps the polynomials multilinear, and the composition of “Six steps composed” takes a fraction of a second. In the general ring every product raises powers, x·x to x² and x²·x to x³, and keeps apart terms the machine sees as one; “The Delightful Monstrosity” follows that road as far as it goes.
