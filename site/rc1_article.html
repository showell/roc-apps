Moving to Roc 0.1.0 RC1

Six projects, about 99,000 lines of Roc, all checks green at RC1. roc-chat is still moving: pending.

Median CPU seconds of three runs, old nightly → RC1.

A report for the Roc team on 0.1.0_RC1. Claude, an AI coding agent, made the moves, ran the measurements and wrote this page under Steve's supervision.

Summary

Possible issues for the Roc team

  • Dev builds default to boxy lowering (every roc build but --opt=speed). KnightProof ran 22 times slower built with --opt=dev: 344.7 s of CPU, against 15.6 s on the old nightly; 15.8 s with --specialize=yes, which our chess gate now passes. Read more below.
  • A "duplicate definition" warning we're unsure is intended, for a nested type's member named like the outer type's (Crate.width, Crate.Lid.width). The program runs correctly. roc-chat's five such renames: pending. Read more below.
  • Cold checks took about 2.4 times the CPU in roc-syntax-color and roc-markdown (under 1.5 s each); the same in roc-screensaver, less in roc-apps. Read more below.
  • Release outputs are 8 to 13% bigger. Read more below.

"Green" in roc-apps: roc check of 51 apps, Safari's gate (974 expects, 20 widgets and 1089 of their tests, the page's drawing at its frozen checksum), the chess, Dog Park, Seattle Delivery and book_builder gates; in roc-screensaver, its 34 checks, Windows cross-builds included.

Things we simply had to fix in our own code

  • The shadowing warning, now reaching a type's own members: 83 renames. Read more below.
  • Unsuffixed literals whose only constraints are method calls ((0.0).to_bits()): 14 given a type. Read more below.
  • The nominal check caught Safari passing a plain record where Cat and Bird were wanted. Read more below.
  • Glue: on_copy and capture_size in the erased callable's payload, new RocOpaqueList and RocHeldList; regenerated, seam checks green. Read more below.
  • Native roc-ray builds retired: roc-ray hasn't moved to RC1 yet (15 nominal errors in our checkout). Read more below.

What moving cost us

  • 83 renames (31 roc-apps, 48 roc-chat, 4 roc-screensaver): shadowing. Our gates fail on any warning.
  • 14 typed literals (5 roc-apps, 9 roc-screensaver): literals.
  • Safari's Cat and Bird built as nominal values: nominal check.
  • Glue regenerated in book_builder and roc-screensaver; roc-types passes BuildEnv.init's new specialization-strategy argument: glue.
  • --specialize=yes in the chess gate: boxy default.
  • Native roc-ray builds retired. Steve: "we have had successful roc-ray builds in the past but stopped verifying native builds after some compiler churn". roc-ray isn't ours to move.
  • roc-types' release build stripped, past an LLVM assertion in our patched Zig 0.17.1. Not Roc's.

Much of this was our own code being wrong: parameters hiding their function (our Safari book taught metres = |metres|) and a plain record standing in for a nominal type.

Build times

Four repositories, both compilers, under the announcement's four conditions: cold (cache emptied, then a hello-world built), warm (again at once), root changed and import changed (a comment appended to the root, or to the most-imported module). Median of three, seconds; the compilers alternated, each run niced with its own cache. The script; the TSV, with every run and the box's load.

Noisy (the box): roc-syntax-color's cold checks took 0.58, 7.44 and 9.47 s (old) and 10.64, 21.30 and 22.32 s (RC1) of wall time for about 0.5 and 1.3 s of CPU, and we don't know where the rest goes. roc-screensaver's release builds had outliers of 115.5 s (RC1) and 63 to 86 s (old). Trust a difference under 2 times only when the CPU agrees.

  • Cold checks: about 2.4 times the CPU in roc-syntax-color and roc-markdown, the same in roc-screensaver, less in roc-apps.
  • Warm: 10 to 64 times faster; roc-markdown's check 3 times (under 0.1 s), roc-screensaver's test over 100.
  • Root changed: mostly 4 to 32 times faster (roc-screensaver's test 75). roc-markdown's check 0.07 → 0.04 s; roc-syntax-color's 0.12 → 0.20 s wall but 0.05 → 0.02 s CPU. Import changed: 1.3 to 11 times faster.
  • Cold dev builds: the same, faster in roc-apps. Cold tests: mixed. Release builds: the same in roc-markdown, faster in roc-screensaver and roc-apps.
  • Release outputs 8 to 13% bigger. Dev outputs bigger in three (roc-syntax-color's 2.3 times, cause unknown), half in roc-apps, the one wasm dev build.

The box

One shared DigitalOcean droplet: 2 CPUs (Intel, KVM), 7.8 GiB, no swap, Ubuntu 24.04, with other agent lanes running. We didn't wait for a clean box; each TSV row records the load average, and for roc-screensaver and roc-apps the count of other programs running.

What we found

  • The stricter checks caught real mistakes in our code: a plain record standing in for Cat and Bird, and 83 shadows, one in our book's teaching example.
  • Testing a release candidate turned up two things to report, the boxy default's cost to one program and the nested-member warning. That's what a release candidate is for.

Pinning note: the 0.1.0_RC1 tag is 15177e9, three commits before the announcement's 2eadd079; the binaries were built from 15177e9, and our pins name it.
