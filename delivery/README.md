# Seattle Delivery

Open the page, press **space**, and watch eight trucks fan out across a
playful, out-of-scale map of Seattle to deliver a hundred grocery orders. It is
a screensaver with an algorithm at its heart: the fun is watching the routes
make sense. On the dev channel it is `/delivery/` (`build.shale`, below).

The keys:

- **space**: play the day; the trucks drive their routes and drop totes,
  then park.
- **S**: shuffle a new shift (a fresh random draw of orders). Each shift is
  numbered, so "that one looked odd on S58" names it.
- **A**: step through the solver itself, one move at a time, to watch the
  plan get built. **←/→** scrub either animation; **B** or **Esc** returns to
  the static map.

Hover a neighborhood to see its name; hover a delivered house to highlight its
truck.

## Under the hood

A small, finished **capacitated vehicle-routing (CVRP)** simulator. Roc owns
the solver, Zig hosts it, TypeScript draws it.

- **Roc** (`roc/`) holds the whole problem: the map and its road graph, the
  day's orders, the trucks and their caps, the cost, construction, local
  search, the race, and the JSON the page reads. `roc/delivery.roc` is the
  app, on `roc/platform/`, and `roc/Solve.roc`'s `routes_traced` is the
  solver's front door.
- **Zig** (`src/`) is the host: `roc_seam.zig` gives the Roc its memory and
  its crashes, `wasm.zig` is the page's `solver.wasm` (`solveShift(seed)` and
  `outPtr()`), and the native judges run the Roc against the gold.
- **TypeScript** is the display, from `main.ts`. `solver.ts` is the readable
  reference solver the gold is minted from (`goldgen.ts`); it stays out of the
  page's bundle.

**Why this seam.** The page asks one question, a shift's plan by its seed, and
the solver needs nothing else from the page. So the Roc takes a seed and
answers the page's JSON, and the Zig carries bytes. The road graph and the gate
angles are float geometry, and they are the Roc's too: every float there is
plain IEEE arithmetic, or `atan2` from Roc's own fdlibm port, the same bits on
every target. The gold pins the road graph (every edge's minutes, every pair's
minutes and path), so any drift fails loudly. The map lives in both Roc and
TypeScript, one for the solver and one for the drawing; the gold's substrate
section holds the two in agreement.

### The algorithm, in one breath

Eight trucks, about a hundred orders, asymmetric capacities (the west side
carries more than the east). The solver builds routes with the
**Clarke-Wright savings heuristic**, then improves them with local search
(2-opt, or-opt, exchange, arc-rebalance). Every move is scored against one
**integer "pain" cost** (`roc/Pain.roc`'s `pain_of`), and that is the
load-bearing trick: every decision compares integers, so the greedy tie-breaks
agree across engines. A four-way construction **race** keeps the minimum.
Almost everything you watch emerges from that cost. One piece of local
knowledge: Bellevue and Medina, across the lake from the fulfillment center,
are overflow neighborhoods, placed in the trucks' spare room at the end (each
neighborhood's `overflow` in `roc/Seattle.roc` says).

The Roc matches `solver.ts` in the order it scans and compares: ties go to the
first candidate everywhere, lists keep JS's Map and Set insertion order, the
float epsilons become integer comparisons (`> 1e-9` is `>= 1`), and each
replay snapshot is taken where the TypeScript takes it (`roc/Replay.roc`,
`roc/Build.roc`'s `force_placed`).

### Building, running, testing

It needs Zig (the version `build.zig.zon` names), the pinned Roc
(`../roc-version`, at `~/tools/roc-<pin>/roc`; the build refuses any other),
node, and `npm ci` in this folder once (tsc and esbuild). From roc-apps' root:

    shale delivery/build.shale    # every check, then the page into the dev channel
    shale delivery/lints.shale    # Shale's Roc name hunt, with delivery's terms (terms.txt)

and, in `delivery/`:

    zig build gold -Droc-opt=dev   # the Roc against the gold, on the fast-to-build backend
    zig build gold                 # the same, as served (LLVM)
    zig build wasm-gold            # the page's own solver.wasm against the gold, under node
    zig build roc-test             # Roc's expects and roc fmt --check roc
    zig build bench                # a shift's solve timed natively
    zig build wasm-bench           # the same in the browser's engine, under node

The gold (`solver_gold.json`, `solver_frames_gold.json`) is regenerated by
`goldgen.ts` (its header has the command) after an intentional change to
`solver.ts`'s semantics. A difference between the Roc and the gold is a bug in
the Roc.

### The hot loop's rules

The solver's speed lives in its hot loop. Keep these rules in any change to
it:

- **A list has one owner at a time.** A `for`, `fold` or `any` over a list,
  and a list a caller keeps while passing it, count the list up and down
  atomically. The pain loop and Route's checks read stops by index, and the
  scans thread their route through `Pain.price_view`.
- **Price a candidate in place.** `Pain.View` reads a route with a run
  reversed or a stop inserted, in place; a scan builds the one candidate it
  keeps.
- **Use `times_wrap` for 64-bit multiplies in the hot loop.** On wasm32 a
  checked multiply is a call to a 128-bit multiply; the pain's rate and the
  tables' indexes have factors of a few hundred at most.
- **One arena a solve** (`src/roc_seam.zig`), a bump pointer reset when the
  next solve starts.
